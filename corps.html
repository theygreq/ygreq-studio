Corps

A long-term documentary project with the Sofia Opera and Ballet Ongoing — 2026–

Statement

Corps is a documentary project made with the ballet company of the Sofia Opera and Ballet. What drew me to it wasn't ballet as a subject, but the dancers — people who turn up every day to do the same thing they did yesterday, better.

I struggle with repetitive, predictable work. A whole cohort of people who don't is magnetic. I was drawn in particular to the corps de ballet — the literal body of a company, tens of people who stay largely unknown to the public. Many will never have their turn in the spotlight, through bad luck, injury, or simply being overlooked. The question I started with was: why do it?

What I found was a group of fiercely disciplined people whose focus on perfection isn't for an audience, or even for each other. Marta Petkova, the company's prima, told me the only person she has ever danced for is herself — you get up every morning and go to battle against yourself. Any day that ends without her feeling she has pushed her limits is a rest day.

There is also far more superstition than I expected. Red threads, small rituals before going on, firm views about which new pointe shoe belongs on which foot. For an art form apparently built around being looked at, it is intensely private.

A photograph can't show any of that directly. What it can show is the ordinary set of conditions the commitment happens inside — scuffed rooms, improvised equipment, endless waiting, a stage that has to be swept dry before anyone can dance on it. Nobody is watching, and they do it anyway.

The work is made with the company rather than about it, and it is not finished. This page shows selected frames from an ongoing edit.

Process

Two kinds of distance. Optical: a long lens on the rehearsal floor, where moving closer would interrupt the work. Physical: a 50mm at the edges of the day — warm-ups, breaks, waiting, shoes being broken in — as trust makes proximity possible. Colour and monochrome are decided frame by frame in post rather than as a house style: monochrome where gesture and grain carry it, colour where colour is information.

Ongoing

The edit is open and the work is continuing. If you are thinking about exhibition, publication, or a collaboration, I would be glad to talk.
